# Plan 03-04: Calibrated Leitner Engine & Clinical Order Verification Station

## Objective

## Scientific & Pedagogical Ground Truth
- Derived from:
  - Critical audit findings: Fixed Leitner interval boxes with $S = \text{interval}$ yield $R = e^{-1} \approx 37\%$ retention at due date, which causes rapid forgetting. Target retention for professional medical/pharmacy licensing exams must maintain $R \ge 0.85$.
- Mathematical Decay Model:
  $$R(t) = e^{-\frac{t}{S}}$$
  To guarantee that retention never falls below $R_{\text{target}} = 0.85$:
  $$t_{\text{due}} = -S \cdot \ln(R_{\text{target}}) = -S \cdot \ln(0.85) \approx 0.1625 \cdot S$$
- Backward Scheduling from Target Exam Date:
  When an exam date $t_{\text{exam}}$ is set:
  $$S_{\text{required}} = \frac{t_{\text{exam}} - t_{\text{now}}}{-\ln(0.85)}$$
  The review engine dynamically scales review frequency based on student test date proximity rather than arbitrary fixed calendar boxes.

## Clinical Order Verification Station
- Interactive clinical simulation component where the student acts as a hospital/clinical verification pharmacist reviewing incoming drug orders:
  1. **Case 1 (Ciprofloxacin + Calcium Carbonate)**:
     - Order: Ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO BID + Calcium Carbonate 1000 mg PO TID with meals.
     - Clinical Trap: Bidentate multivalent cation chelation reduces fluoroquinolone AUC by $\sim 40\text{--}50\%$.
     - Correct Action: Intervene $\to$ Space oral calcium by $\ge 2$ hours after or $\ge 6$ hours before ciprofloxacin.
  2. **Case 2 (Simvastatin + Clarithromycin)**:
     - Order: Simvastatin 40 mg PO QHS + Clarithromycin 500 mg PO BID for acute bronchitis.
     - Clinical Trap: Clarithromycin is a potent mechanism-based CYP3A4 inhibitor, causing a 12-fold (+1100%) surge in simvastatin lactone AUC, triggering myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.
     - Correct Action: Intervene $\to$ Suspend simvastatin during macrolide therapy or substitute non-CYP3A4 antibiotic/statin (e.g. pravastatin, azithromycin).
  3. **Case 3 (Warfarin Initiation & Heparin Bridging)**:
     - Order: Acute DVT patient; start Warfarin 5 mg PO daily and stop Enoxaparin immediately upon day 2 INR of 1.8.
     - Clinical Trap: INR rises early due to short half-life of Factor VII ($t_{1/2} \approx 6\text{ h}$), but true antithrombotic efficacy requires depletion of Factor II ($t_{1/2} \approx 60\text{--}72\text{ h}$) and Factor X ($t_{1/2} \approx 36\text{--}48\text{ h}$). Halting heparin early leaves the patient hypercoagulable (transient Protein C depletion, $t_{1/2} \approx 8\text{ h}$).
     - Correct Action: Intervene $\to$ Continue therapeutic parenteral anticoagulation bridge for $\ge 5$ days AND until INR is therapeutic ($\ge 2.0$) for $\ge 24\text{ hours}$.
